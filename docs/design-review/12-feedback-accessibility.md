# Feedback, world presentation, and accessibility adversarial review

Reviewer scope: `World.tsx`, `Hairline.tsx`, `Network.tsx`, `App.tsx`, `Settings.tsx`, `feedback.ts`, `useGame.ts`, CSS, and the three public Hairline figures and their shared kernel. This review did not change production code, operate a user save, or conduct a human playtest.

## Research actually retrieved

All sources below returned HTTP 200 and their relevant text was read on 2026-10-04. The MDA PDF was retrieved and extracted with `pdftotext`. Accessibility guidance supplies requirements and failure mechanisms, not evidence that a particular game is enjoyable. MDA supplies an analytical vocabulary, not a controlled study or an optimal tuning formula.

## Blocking findings sent to the implementation owner

1. **Continuously changing ETA inside a live region.** `Network.tsx` places the bottleneck and the ticking completion estimate in one `role="status"` container. `useGame.ts` rerenders every 250ms; the displayed clock can change every second. A screen reader can repeatedly announce time estimates while the player reads or operates controls. Keep the estimate visible but outside the live region. Live-announce a bottleneck only when its category changes. This follows the W3C status-message goal of useful updates without unnecessary interruption. A markup regression test should establish that ETA is outside the status region, while browser assistive-technology testing remains necessary.

2. **Meaningful timestamp text misses minimum contrast.** `.company-log time` and `.figure-read` use `#777` on white. Computed sRGB contrast is approximately 4.478:1, below 4.5:1. Timestamps convey information and are 10px text. Use `#666` or black for these text rules. The inactive button rule is exempt and does not justify weakening meaningful text. The figure readout is supplementary but can receive the same repair with no aesthetic cost.

These findings are concrete, independently grounded objections. They are not judgments that a monochrome or quiet game needs more effects.

## Acceptance and uncertainty

The researched rationale supports supplementary world animation, optional sounds, textual alternatives, reduced motion, visible focus, and restrained announcements. It does not prove enjoyment, stickiness, award readiness, or that the exact art gates and timings are optimal.

The two blocking findings must be fixed and re-reviewed. The lower-priority risks need honest limits: browser touch/focus/screen-reader testing, zoom and small-text readability, quiet first-time playthrough, and performance measurements were not completed by this reviewer. A marketing claim of broad accessibility would be premature.
