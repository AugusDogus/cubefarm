# Projects, progression, and discovery adversarial review

Scope: all 19 project definitions, their authorization and reveal rules, objective navigation, and supply/demand discovery in the Office. Reviewed 2026-10-04 against `src/game/projects.ts`, `objectives.ts`, `discovery.ts`, `economy.ts`, `strategy.ts`, `story.ts`, `App.tsx`, `components/Projects.tsx`, and `components/OfficeGrowth.tsx`. No production changes are owned by this review.

## Sources actually retrieved and read

1. Jakob Nielsen, [Progressive Disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/), retrieved 2026-10-04. The article says to show a few important options initially, defer specialized options, and make the way to the secondary controls obvious. Its explicit qualification is to disclose frequently needed features up front. It also warns that splitting interdependent tasks across stages creates excessive navigation. This is a usability principle, not a controlled experiment on incremental games or evidence for a specific revenue threshold.
2. Frank Lantz, [Universal Paperclips](https://www.decisionproblem.com/paperclips/), with the actual [main.js](https://www.decisionproblem.com/paperclips/main.js) and [projects.js](https://www.decisionproblem.com/paperclips/projects.js?v3), retrieved 2026-10-04. `manageProjects()` separately evaluates a project's `trigger()` to display it and `cost()` to enable it. Improved AutoClippers appears after one clipmaker but requires 750 operations. WireBuyer appears after 15 wire purchases and costs 7,000 operations. This establishes a real game precedent for revealing a solution after encountering its problem and showing the cost before affordability. It does not establish that 15 purchases, 19 projects, or Cube Farm's timings are optimal.

The judgment below uses these sources adversarially: minimal UI is useful only when it still lets the player find the action that solves the visible problem.

## Reveal decisions and their limits

- **A fresh game has no projects or Development navigation.** Development is revealed at $100 lifetime revenue, or retained when any project is already recorded. Time study's generic card would otherwise reveal at $60 because of the 60% rule. The navigation gate effectively makes its normal discovery $100. Retain the narrow opening. NN/g supports focusing attention; Paperclips supplies a direct staged-game precedent. Neither proves $100 is the best moment.
- **A generic project card appears only after all prerequisite projects, in the current or an earlier era, at 60% of its revenue gate.** Completed projects and the unchosen permanent management path are removed from the active list. The 60% factor provides advance notice of the cost and destination without a complete future tree. Retain early notice provisionally. No retrieved source justifies 60%; test anticipation time rather than call the number scientifically grounded.
- **Cash, insight, influence, correspondence and staff gates do not hide an otherwise revealed card.** They disable authorization and state the first unmet condition. This correctly separates discovery from affordability, as Paperclips does. Showing only the first unmet condition reduces clutter but can conceal the total requirements until the player satisfies earlier ones. Descriptions partly mitigate this for charter/regional/sovereign. A complete compact requirement list would be easier to plan, but should be validated before increasing UI density.
- **Old optional projects remain available after era transitions.** Prior systems move to Archive at Network. This prevents a one-time phase transition from deleting purchases, but those archived purchases have less relevance. Retain save safety and avoid forcing archival management as a Network chore.
- **Completed projects live in a collapsed list.** Retain because the active area should expose pending decisions. The text list is a record rather than a full replayable description. A player who forgot a project's effect may need more information later; this is a minor reference-UX limitation, not a softlock.
- **Objectives list a chosen milestone path rather than every optional project.** Office prioritizes Brand if output/backlog is demand-limited. Enterprise skips optional cultivation and logistics. This reduces competing goals and keeps genetics optional. Retain a suggested path, but label it as guidance, not the only valid strategy. The Enterprise list also skips the management choice even though Regional requires it; the Regional blocker must provide an actionable route.
- **The project list can grow within an era.** Nine Enterprise projects are possible in the catalog, with prerequisites and mutually exclusive paths reducing the simultaneous set. There is no evidence that this list size is cognitively optimal. A minimal fresh start does not justify a busy midgame; novice and returning-player tests must check whether project purpose and priority remain understandable.

## Supply and demand discovery in the reported 18-person save

The user reported $556.69, 18 headquarters workers, output 17.9/s, demand 15/s at $0.65, with office upgrades and incentives but no visible procurement or marketing. We cannot claim to have inspected that browser or infer its exact project/revenue state from these numbers.

The current code puts **Automate blank-form purchases** beside supplies and **Grow customer demand** beside pricing. Both expose Time study directly when missing, list the dependent project, and use the actual authorization reason. Low stock opens the supply explanation. Excess expected output or a large inventory opens demand help. Marketing states its +60% demand effect at the same price. After Time study, the explanation points to sales staffing below. This is the right corrective structure under NN/g's caveat about frequently needed features: an essential bottleneck solution should be discoverable where the problem is visible, not merely somewhere in the tree.

At the posted cash, the $80 Time study and $90 procurement are affordable before considering earned insights, but authorization still needs the $100/$200 lifetime gates. The $250 Brand book is affordable independently and requires 15 insights plus its $300 lifetime gate. After Time study the base continuous review produces about 0.2967 insights/s before bonuses: 10 insights takes about 34 seconds and 15 about 51 seconds with no dedicated researchers. The hint explains continuous income and how to speed it up. This is not a circular resource dependency. Assigning sales is a reversible immediate demand lever once Time study exists, without waiting for Brand.

The static rendering test `supply-demand.test.tsx` checks that the Office includes these prerequisites, relief and precise prices. That test proves text presence, not visibility in the user's separate browser, attention, comprehension, or usefulness under time pressure. A stale app build would also lack the new controls; this review makes no claim about the browser's loaded version.

## Findings that should change implementation

1. **Objective buttons need blocker-specific navigation.** `Objectives.tsx` always labels and routes an actionable target to Development. Regional's acquisition gate and Sovereign's branch gate are solved in Company. Charter's employee gate is solved in Office. Letter gates are solved in correspondence. The current title/detail may tell the truth while the button sends the player away from the solution. Route to the blocker location, with an accurate label, and retain Development when authorization or research resources are the next task. This is a concrete contradiction of the source's requirement that navigation have clear information scent.
2. **Do not pretend exact thresholds were individually justified by the aggregate playthrough.** Existing formula-informed runs show Enterprise around 11-12 minutes, Conglomerate around 18-21 minutes and Network around 20-27 minutes. They automatically inspect every visible project and attempt purchases, so they are unusually good at discovering prerequisites. They cannot demonstrate that a human would notice procurement, choose sales, or understand the management gate. Keep the constants as provisional tuning, add novice/returning-player evidence, and record uncertainty.

## Circularity and recovery verdict

No circular project prerequisite was found. Time study purchases no insights; research then has a passive floor. Charter purchases no influence; after charter, influence has a passive floor as well as sales/contracts. Analytics unlocks cultivation before its reply gate becomes relevant. Standards triggers Robin's promise before charter requires the reply. Mergers permits two acquisitions before Regional requires them. Regional permits three branches and additional acquisitions before Sovereign requires them. There are six rivals, so the four-market gate does not demand unavailable content.

Staff removal can delay charter's six-person requirement but does not erase learned controls. Lifetime revenue and hire history retain discovered navigation. Manual emergency work and supplier relief are separate recovery mechanisms. These observations establish reachable resources and prerequisites, not immunity to every poor economic decision.

Reviewer disposition: retain staged progression and contextual supply/demand discovery; require accurate objective navigation; keep all exact numerical values explicitly provisional pending imperfect-player and human evidence. No award-quality, enjoyment, or addictiveness conclusion follows from this audit.
