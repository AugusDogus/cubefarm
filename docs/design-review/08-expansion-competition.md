# Adversarial review: expansion and competition

Reviewer: independent expansion/competition agent. Audit date: 2026-10-04. Scope: Enterprise and Conglomerate, contracts, acquisitions, branches, market standing, regulatory pressure, and related transitions. This is a critical audit, not a finding that the game is enjoyable.

## Research actually retrieved

- [Hunicke, LeBlanc, Zubek, MDA (2004)](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/MDA.pdf). Retrieved HTTP 200; extracted PDF text. The paper distinguishes mechanics, dynamics, and intended aesthetic experience. Relevant short claims: competitive play needs "clear feedback about who is winning"; "dramatic tension" needs rising tension, release, and denouement; design and tuning require a formal iterative approach. This supports evaluating what expansion makes the player do and feel. It does not validate a dollar cost or branch count.
- [Ian Schreiber, Level 16: Game Balance (2009)](https://gamedesignconcepts.wordpress.com/2009/08/20/level-16-game-balance/). Retrieved HTTP 200 and read article text. A dominant strategy turns unused alternatives into "extraneous noise" and can create "false decisions". Cost curves consider explicit costs and opportunity costs; mathematical balance still needs playtesting. This directly challenges claims that six acquisition buttons or eight identical branches imply six or eight meaningful strategies.
- [Universal Paperclips live implementation](https://www.decisionproblem.com/paperclips/main.js). Retrieved HTTP 200. Around lines 1260-1330, `humanFlag` hides business, manufacturing and trust, and `spaceFlag` hides factory/harvester/wire-drone controls while revealing probe systems. This is primary evidence that its eras replace the foreground problem and UI, rather than indefinitely accumulating controls. It is an observed implementation, not a general design law or permission to copy its numerical tuning.

An initially guessed Schreiber "Level 6" URL returned HTTP 404. The correct article above was retrieved successfully. No source is being cited as proof of Cube Farm's exact constants.

## Main findings and disposition

1. **Enterprise changes decisions materially.** Headquarters output must compete with research, sales and compliance staffing. Contracts escrow production that otherwise goes to retail. Reversible policies differ from permanent management paths. Retain this structure. The opportunity costs are observable in the engine, rather than additional resource labels alone.
2. **Conglomerate primarily changes scale and staffing granularity.** Branches introduce bulk cohorts with inherited profiles, shared facilities, maintenance and the choice between opening markets and expanding existing capacity. This is defensible as a short scale transition before Network, but should not be described as territory strategy. North/East/etc. are names with identical rules. A second branch adds market reach; expanding a branch adds capacity without market reach. That distinction prevents every expansion button being identical, but geography itself adds no choice.
3. **The three-branch Network prerequisite can be met by empty offices.** `projectReason('sovereign')` checks `branches.length`, not staffing or productivity. If three productive offices are an intended strategic accomplishment, the code does not enforce it. Recommendation: decide explicitly between an office-footprint gate and a productive-footprint gate. Do not silently add another mandatory staffing chore. A populated gate requires displayed criteria and unchanged historical-save acceptance.
4. **Acquisitions are an ascending ladder, not six competing specializations.** Every target adds the same `+1.5` market coefficient while prices and staff counts increase. Cheap-first acquisitions are strongly favored for the mandatory two/four-market gates. Retain as an authored acquisition ladder if stated honestly. Do not claim six independent strategic paths. Alternate target bonuses would need new pacing evidence, not speculative feature growth.
5. **Contract acceptance hides the most useful risk information.** The UI states quantity and deadline but gives no expected completion estimate or required production rate. The automated contract desk already checks estimated output with a 20% safety margin. Recommendation: show required forms/s at the selected 50% allocation, estimated completion and a supply warning before acceptance. Keep the player free to take risks. Do not disable contracts merely because a prediction says they may fail.
6. **Market standing is a passive milestone.** It ranks lifetime gross revenue against deterministic linear rival growth, and acquisition removes rivals from the denominator. This meets the requested non-interactive stock-market standing. It is not a valuation model or adversarial competitor simulation. Explain the metric and keep it secondary, especially because acquisitions can improve rank without adding acquired lifetime revenue to the player's total.
7. **Regulation can constrain extraction without creating a hard failure.** Pressure lowers output and increases Network stabilization cost; compliance, policy and stewardship counteract it. Output is floored and crisis decisions wait indefinitely. This supports recoverable management tension. Whether humane and lean policies remain useful throughout the run is not proven by successful completion tests alone.

## Evidence that exists, and evidence still missing

`progression.test.ts` checks contract escrow, one-time reward settlement, cancellation/timeout returns, legal phase gates, rivalry growth, cohort merging/capacity, pressure recovery, and legal completion of both management routes. `BALANCE.md` reports Enterprise at 11.1-12.1 minutes, Conglomerate at17.7-20.6, and Network at20.4-27.1 across six formula-aware runs. This supports the interpretation of Conglomerate as a short transition rather than a separate hour-long territory game.

Missing evidence: first-time player contract mistakes; whether people recognize the demand benefit of opening a branch; late-game scrolling cost; comparative policy/acquisition strategies under imperfect decisions; whether a finite ascending ladder feels satisfying rather than obligatory. Neither MDA nor Schreiber validates the exact values above. These are explicit authorial parameters awaiting playtesting.

## Recommended smallest implementation follow-through

1. Add manual contract feasibility and supply information. Reuse existing expected-output calculations, disclose approximation, keep acceptance available.
2. Correct Logistics material-efficiency wording and branch production display if their intended effects remain price reduction/global output boosts.
3. Make the Sovereign three-branch gate's intention explicit. If productive footprint is required, include a visible staffing criterion, test empty branches are insufficient and retain historical completed phase saves. If office records are the intention, document that as a pacing prerequisite rather than an earned production challenge.
4. Record rivals and branch geography as authored milestone/capacity structures. Additional region mechanics are not warranted just to justify labels.
5. Have human players compare opening versus upgrading and retail versus contracts. Report observed behavior before claiming strategy diversity or award-quality depth.
