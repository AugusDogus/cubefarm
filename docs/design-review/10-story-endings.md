# Story, ethical commitments, endings, and reincorporation

Reviewer: independent narrative adversary. Scope: all material narrative decisions in `src/game/story.ts`, their economic effects in `conditions.ts` and `economy.ts`, project gates, correspondence presentation, endings, and legacy rewards. This review describes inspected implementation on 2026-10-04. Recommendations are not implementation claims.

## Research actually retrieved

1. Robin Hunicke, Marc LeBlanc, Robert Zubek, *MDA: A Formal Approach to Game Design and Game Research* (2004). Retrieved the complete PDF, HTTP 200, and read its text: <https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/MDA.pdf>. The paper distinguishes mechanics, resulting dynamics, and desired emotional experiences. Its aesthetic vocabulary includes narrative, discovery, and expression. It states that there is no general formula for fun and that tuning requires iterative playtesting. Application: justify this story by the experience its mechanics support, not by counting letters or features. Limitation: a design framework, not an empirical validation of Cube Farm or its constants.
2. Emily Short, *Casual Games and Storylets, or How to Make Game Mechanics Express Choice* (2020-01-09). Retrieved HTTP 200 and read the article: <https://emshort.blog/2020/01/09/casual-games-and-storylets-or-how-to-make-game-mechanics-express-choice/>. Short discusses allowing players to consume story and gameplay at different rates, framing mechanical behavior as narrative choice, and giving perceptible consequences. Her cautionary example says players did not notice hidden character effects because the game did not frame them as choices. Application: optional letters can wait; irreversible replies need visible consequences; staffing and firing could carry narrative meaning without adding another moral meter. Limitation: practitioner analysis, including commercial casual games, not proof that narrative gating is enjoyable here.
3. Emily Short, *What Does Your Narrative System Need to Do?* (2022-04-09). Retrieved HTTP 200 and read the article: <https://emshort.blog/2022/04/09/what-does-your-narrative-system-need-to-do/>. It separates content selection, option management, consequence resolution, and presentation. It notes that new options can communicate consequences without expensive large branches, and that complex deterministic results need sufficient explanation. Application: audit letter triggers, lab/charter gates, economic effects, and ending echoes separately. Limitation: a taxonomy and examples, not a requirement to add branching or a numerical balance rule.
4. Emily Short, *Loose, Tight, Flat, and Bumpy Stats in ChoiceScript Games* (2017-05-09). Retrieved HTTP 200 and read the article: <https://emshort.blog/2017/05/09/loose-tight-flat-and-bumpy-stats-in-choicescript-games/>. It evaluates consequences relative to the state distribution of players who reach a choice, recommends tracking major narrative events explicitly, and warns about outcomes requiring near-perfect execution or wiki guides. It also discusses whether players can perceive the value of percentages. Application: the $250,000 settlement must be judged against endgame wealth; explicit promise/consent flags are appropriate; both endings should remain available without guide-perfect early decisions. Limitation: its numerical examples concern ChoiceScript stats, not idle economies.

Unavailable candidates were not used as evidence: guessed Emily Short and Failbetter article URLs returned 404; candidate copies of *Agency Reconsidered* failed TLS, connection, or DNS. No claim below relies on those unread sources. Research supports principles and questions. It does not establish the exact choice percentages, 16-letter count, billion-workflow pool, or five-credit reward.

## Highest-priority objections

1. **Ending correspondence is actionable but rejected.** `App.tsx` always renders `Correspondence`; its non-choice ending letters offer `File this letter`. `engine.ts` rejects every ending-phase action except `reincorporate` and `pause`. A player who saved letters cannot archive them once the finale is selected. Allow `read-letter` after ending, preserving the frozen economy and rejecting economic actions. Add a semantic regression for an unread finished/queued letter after both endings. This is a concrete defect, not subjective narrative preference.
2. **The finite-work premise conflicts with the continuing retail economy.** `tickEconomy` continues generating and selling ordinary Office forms throughout Network, then also clears the billion-workflow pool. Letters and finale claim every remaining workflow has been located and no forms remain. Prior economy is valuable for funding and historical continuity, so rewriting it is disproportionate. Clarify that the finite pool is inherited/legacy obligations and that selecting a finale retires further retail obligations. Ending should describe a deliberate change in institution purpose, rather than a simulation proof that all conceivable human work has disappeared.
3. **The patent settlement is a symbolic consequence, not an economic obstacle.** `commonsSettlement` charges $250,000 only for patents. The report explicitly acknowledges this is symbolic against late wealth. For example, $250,000 is 0.0625% of $400 million. Retain as restitution if clearly labeled. Do not claim this balances patent output or creates a difficult final sacrifice. Increasing the cost retroactively would be an unsupported punishment of saved choices.
4. **Bundled path benchmarks do not validate individual replies.** The tested stewardship route also takes voice/consent, humane policy, and a research genome; extraction takes quota/patent, lean policy, and a production genome. Six successful runs prove those bundles can finish. They do not isolate whether a particular promise or patent choice is worthwhile. Use matched states and policies to compare the ongoing effects, and classify exact constants as tuning hypotheses until human evidence exists.

## Acceptance evidence and remaining uncertainty

The existing tests prove permanent replies cannot be reversed, story waits offline, legal bundled paths reach both endings, settlement is applied once, reincorporation grants five credits, and old worker traits persist. They do not prove human attachment, dramatic payoff, nuanced ethics, or repeat-play value.

Recommended smallest additional checks: ending-phase letter filing and finished-letter eligibility; Robin label after transfer versus firing; first-team text after firing/re-hiring; all reply/ending echo combinations; matched-state economic sensitivity for each commitment under constrained and unconstrained demand; explicit reincorporation reset copy. Preserve old letter IDs and reply state, and do not add hidden ending requirements to historical saves.

Human playtest questions: Did players understand they were cultivating people before selecting genes? Could they describe their first promise and perceive any consequence? Did a patent owner experience the final buyout as restitution rather than a meaningful financial sacrifice? Did the finale feel like release, an arbitrary stop, or a moral lecture? Did late letters interrupt planning or help make the institution feel inhabited? Do not substitute completion time or an adversary's approval for those answers.
