# Adversarial review: office investments and memos

Reviewer: independent facilities reviewer. Scope: office upgrades, facilities, capacity, memos, purchase estimates and their consequences for existing and future workers. Reviewed 2026-10-04. This document reviews the implementation before the audit fixes. Recommendations remain open until the integration ledger records their disposition.

## Research actually retrieved

These sources were fetched over HTTPS during this review. They justify methods and principles, not Cube Farm's exact constants.

- **R1. Ian Schreiber, [Level 3: Transitive Mechanics and Cost Curves](https://gamebalanceconcepts.wordpress.com/2010/07/21/level-3-transitive-mechanics-and-cost-curves/), 2010.** An increasing cost curve can encourage players to explore other options. The appropriate curve depends on intended length, turns and flow. His instruction to make a complete list of costs and benefits informs the inventory below. This is practitioner teaching, not experimental evidence that 1.8 is ideal.
- **R2. Ian Schreiber, [Level 6: Situational Balance](https://gamebalanceconcepts.wordpress.com/2010/08/11/level-6-situational-balance/), 2010.** Benefits whose value changes with game state need expected-value analysis and playtesting. Prerequisite costs must be amortized across purchases they enable. This directly challenges judging coffee or QA by nominal percentage alone.
- **R3. Ian Schreiber, [Level 7: Advancement, Progression and Pacing](https://gamebalanceconcepts.wordpress.com/2010/08/18/level-7-advancement-progression-and-pacing/), 2010.** He identifies endgames that merely go through motions and recommends new mechanics or shortening that stage. Office chores should retire when the game changes scale.
- **R4. Robin Hunicke, Marc LeBlanc and Robert Zubek, [MDA: A Formal Approach to Game Design and Game Research](https://www.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/MDA.pdf), 2004.** Retrieved PDF and extracted text. The paper calls for both qualitative and quantitative iteration and warns that components cannot be evaluated apart from their effects on the system and player experience. It explicitly rejects a universal formula for fun.

The Game Developer idle-game math article returned HTTP 403 to this reviewer. It is not evidence retrieved here. None of these sources establish that the numerical settings below are award quality or addictive.

## Marginal-value evidence

Computed with current `expectedOutput`, `sustainableIncome`, `marginalPurchase` and legal fixture hires using deterministic random0.99 (aptitude1.147), business-as-usual climate, fixed morale60, no contracts, no genes, plentiful supply. Unconstrained scenarios set marketing10 to isolate production return. Costs are exact current code prices. This is analytical verification, not a human playtest.

At 36 workers/equipment4, cafeteria payoff233.1s, gym588.0s, benefits487.0s before morale recovery. These investments can be productive while higher repeated risk-control upgrades are poor HQ-only purchases. The three-branch row uses a legal bounded fixture with centralization, franchise and infrastructure, no genes, normal retail price$1.25 and marketing25 solely to remove the sales bottleneck. It validates the shared scaling effect, not whether that marketing investment is financially attractive in a real run. Branch economies radically reverse upper-level value; these findings do not prove upper levels globally worthless.

An18-worker scenario with equipment3/training2/QA2, coffee/snacks/cafeteria/gym, no marketing, price$0.65 has expected output20.166/s and demand19.469/s. This approximates the user's purchasing situation but does not reproduce their employee aptitudes, climate or saved reputation. Equipment's next level lowers sustainable cash income by$0.202/s; training by$0.008/s; QA by$0.004/s; benefits by$0.034/s. A90-second baseline memo lowers cash by$78.08 including its$20 charge, because additional forms consume supplies without additional retail revenue. Initial backlog does not solve continuous oversupply.

## Verification limits and requested follow-through

Existing tests establish improvement effects, exact upgrade accounting, immutable traits, capped capacity, memo duration, reserve handling and early-versus-large investment priority. They do not establish that all five incentives or upper upgrade levels are interesting choices. `BALANCE.md` demonstrates formula-aware players complete the game; its purchase player usually stops risk controls around level7, so the completion test is not a validation of cap-level value.

Before claiming the design is justified, integration must resolve inaccurate descriptions and demand-blind memo repetition. Retained constants must be labeled hypotheses and tracked through imperfect purchase policies or human sessions. Evidence supports a coherent loop with situational investments; it does not support a claim of proven enjoyment.
