# Final economy adversarial review

The final review uses the 24-times final procedure multiplier at its unchanged 25,000 knowledge cost. The implementation charges preparation upfront, protects the player's explicit knowledge reserve for manual tenders, and retains next-protocol protection for automatic Research first commissioning. A bid can consume knowledge intended for the next protocol, which must remain explicit in the desk's warning. Legacy committed quotes remain cash-only through their saved terms version. The supplied save was read for simulation only and remains unchanged.

This resolves the hard-reserve problem identified below: the optional desk can now provide useful early research and cannot bypass scarcity simply because headquarters has accumulated billions. Preparation costs at full exposure are 6 insights in Enterprise, 40 in Conglomerate, and 175 knowledge in Network. Losses consume that preparation as well as the cash exposure.

## Expected returns and remaining limits

Enumerating all legal priority profiles at full matched exposure gives expected net research of 8.358–11.633 insights (Enterprise), 55.721–77.551 insights (Conglomerate), and 243.778–339.287 knowledge (Network), after preparation. Actual losing outcomes are respectively minus 6, minus 40, and minus 175 research. The best approach changes with ordinary priority profiles and client identity no longer determines the correct approach.

Expected returns remain positive and effectively proportional to exposure. Maximum exposure therefore remains best for a player maximizing expectation and accepting the research risk, while smaller exposure reduces actual loss magnitude and protects planned purchases. The game now has genuine losses in a useful resource, but it should not claim that every exposure size has an equally optimal strategy niche. This is a conventional optional positive-expectation minigame, not a complete solution to late-game strategic depth.

Disposition: preparation and explicit-reserve changes address the discovered wealth and accessibility problems without making tenders mandatory. Retain the loss warning, exact paid/net receipts, committed outcomes, and manual approach/bid selection. Do not lengthen waiting to manufacture difficulty. Human playtesting is still needed to judge whether inspecting the briefs and watching their outcomes remains enjoyable after several repetitions.

## Superseded cash-only baseline

## Earlier cash-only tender and Network coordination review

Reviewed the implemented `tender-state`, `tender`, `network`, `network-capital`, and `network-investment` modules. This is a quantitative design review, not a claim about human enjoyment. No production changes or test weakening were made.

## Tender approach choices work; exposure loses meaning with wealth

Enumerating all 693 legal priority profiles produces a different highest cash expectation across approaches: Assurance 195 profiles, Responsive 231, Ambitious 267. Thus reading priorities has a measurable consequence.

Every profile has a profitable highest-expectation approach. At the Enterprise $2,000 cap, expected cash profit ranges from $393.02 to $938.78, or 19.65% to 46.94% of exposure. Both cash expectation and scarce-resource rewards scale linearly with exposure. Once cash is abundant, the economically informed choice is therefore the maximum exposure on every available brief. Smaller exposure protects short-term liquidity but has no competing mechanical advantage.

A legal fresh stewardship run, seed 7, reaches Network at 1,464 seconds with $3,065,308.50 cash and estimated headquarters income of $147,360.91/s. The Network $500,000 cap represents approximately 3.4 seconds of ongoing headquarters income. The submitted historical treasury of $1.65 billion makes this risk smaller still. Calling the loss bounded is correct; calling it a consequential late-game sacrifice would overstate the implementation.

This does not mean the tender makes completion trivial. The useful early reward is knowledge, while losing cash often has little Network consequence. A follow-up should use the era's scarce resource or an explicitly temporary department commitment as the high-exposure cost, rather than trying to fix wealth with a longer cooldown or arbitrary larger dollar numbers. Such a change requires fresh and historical-run validation and explicit UI disclosure. Until then, describe this as an optional randomized research opportunity, not a strong late-game risk-management mechanic.

## Network constraints

Cleared-work support constrains natural replication before Form compression. The 250-workflows-per-office credit cost creates an actual production-versus-expansion relationship. Once compression increases processing, this constraint can cease binding, which provides a real payoff to the protocol.

Survey reduces baseline processing to 65%; Parallel reduces baseline discovery to 65% and spends knowledge for extra processing. These losses persist with abundant compute and cash, so the routes have stronger tradeoffs than the previous additive bonuses. Standard also avoids the intensive-routing contribution to coordination pressure.

Commissioning spends the knowledge needed by protocols and adds coordination load. The protocol opportunity cost is significant early. Coordination itself is generally negligible for small batches: ten offices add only 0.004 load, repaired by ordinary Grow stabilization in roughly one to two seconds. It matters when combined natural growth and commissioning exceed approximately six to eleven offices per second, depending on stabilization and intensive routing. It should not be described as creating a serious management dilemma at eight to one hundred offices.

Commissioning deliberately bypasses cleared-work support. This is safe only while its knowledge and coordination costs are meaningful. Existing headquarters income continues to pay its cash costs, so an economic phase-transition claim would be false. Stronger era transformation remains an unresolved design opportunity, rather than evidence that the current costs make the Network universally difficult.

## Disposition

- Keep changing client priorities and explicit approach selection.
- Keep tender outcomes committed before save and no automatic submission.
- Keep no-tender progression viable. These legal controllers establish that property for the measured entry.
- Do not increase waiting to create difficulty.
- Treat late-game exposure choice as an unresolved weakness, not a passed adversarial requirement.
- Prefer a bounded scarce-resource or department cost for a future high-exposure choice, with a visible refund/reward schedule.
- Do not equate successful simulations with fun, awards, or Universal Paperclips parity.
